# Enforcement tiers: FPPC and SF Ethics Commission

2026-10-28. This note backs the voter guide's conduct rule, under which minor or streamlined outcomes are listed unflagged and full findings or admitted violations are flagged.

## FPPC (state)

The FPPC staff memo of Aug 10, 2026, which proposes amendments to Regulations 18360.1–18360.3, describes five ways to resolve a case, from lightest to heaviest:

1. **Warning Letter.** The case closes with no fine. Staff can issue one under the Commission's general authority, and no stipulation is required. The 2026 amendments drop a duplicate route that issued warning letters through a Streamline stipulation.
2. **Political Reform Education Program (PREP).** The respondent completes training instead of paying a fine.
3. **Tier One Streamline penalty.**
4. **Tier Two Streamline penalty.** This tier was added in the 2020–21 revision.
   - Both Streamline tiers cover "low-level" violations, such as late statements, ad disclaimers, recordkeeping, and late 24-hour reports.
   - Base penalties come from the ranges in Reg. 18360.3 and move in $100 steps.
   - The Chief of Enforcement approves these stipulations. They go to the Commission "for discussion only" in the Executive Staff Report, without a vote.
   - Eligibility uses dollar thresholds. One example is total activity under $10,000 for Tier One and under $25,000 for Tier Two in bank-account cases.
5. **Mainline penalty.** Footnote 2 of the memo sets out three differences from Streamline:
   - fines are higher, "up to $5,000 per count" (Gov. Code 83116(c));
   - the stipulation includes a full narrative of the facts;
   - the Commission must approve it by vote.

   Cases that do not settle go to an administrative hearing and default or adopted decision. This note did not research that path in detail.

**Mapping to the guide:**

| FPPC outcome | Guide treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Warning Letter, PREP, Tier One or Tier Two Streamline | Unflagged |
| Mainline stipulation, decision and order; default decision; court judgment | Flagged |

Source: FPPC, "Adoption of Proposed Amendments to Regulations 18360.1–18360.3," staff memo, Aug 10, 2026, https://www.fppc.ca.gov/siteassets/documents/agendadocuments/general-items/2026/aug/staff_memo_18360.1-18360.3_adopt.pdf

## SF Ethics Commission (local)

1. **Warning Letter.** These are confidential and carry no penalty, under both SARP and regular enforcement. Factors that support one include:
   - a respondent with little experience;
   - self-reporting, or fixing the problem before Enforcement makes contact;
   - minimal public harm;
   - modest fundraising or spending;
   - no warning letter for the same type of violation in the past five years.
2. **Streamlined Administrative Resolution Program (SARP).** The Commission adopted it in February 2021 to expand its 2013 Fixed Penalty Policy, and revised it on March 13, 2026.
   - Fines follow fixed formulas from the SARP Penalty Tables, and they rise the longer a case takes to settle. For example, an incorrectly named committee costs $500, $700 or $900 per reporting period, depending on whether it settles within 30, 45 or 60 days.
   - The respondent signs a streamlined stipulation before staff make a probable cause determination.
   - The Commission ratifies it at a public meeting.
   - The penalty is "generally… less severe than regular enforcement," and the case write-up may be only a summary.
   - A case is excluded from SARP if it involves intent to violate or conceal, a failure to cooperate, a prior penalty for the same type of violation within five years, or more than minimal public harm.
3. **Regular enforcement.** This is a full stipulation, decision and order, or a hearing after a probable cause finding.
   - Penalties are proportional to the violation and higher than SARP's, with a full description of the facts.
   - The maximum under Charter §C3.699-13 is $5,000 per violation or three times the amount not reported or unlawfully given or received, whichever is greater. I took this from Commission documents, not the Charter text itself.

**Mapping to the guide:**

| SF Ethics outcome | Guide treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Warning Letter, SARP stipulation | Unflagged |
| Regular stipulation, decision and order; hearing decision | Flagged |

## Tension to resolve

Streamline and SARP stipulations, at both agencies, include the respondent's acknowledgment of the violation. A literal reading of "admitted violation" would flag them. The README instead lists them unflagged as the agency's minor or streamlined tier, and this guide follows the README. Example: Matt Haney's 2021 SARP stipulation for $1,000 is listed unflagged. This was a policy call made before looking at the data. If it changes, change it for every candidate at once.
