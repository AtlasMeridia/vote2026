## 2026-10-29 — Step 2: what does "impact" mean?

Kenny's framing: he wants items ranked by consequence and by how much they affect him. Local measures may touch him directly. National and hot-button issues move public mood, and mood moves culture. His working idea is that money comes before public mood.

The AI split impact into three lenses (Direct, Structural, Signal; see `method.md` Step 2) and read the official summary and cost estimate for all 25 measures before ranking.

### Finding: the state tax fight

Prop 40 (one-time 5% tax on billionaires' net worth) collides with:

- Prop 42: bans new state taxes on personal property, including financial assets. Cancels conflicting taxes passed after Jan 1, 2026.
- Prop 41: cancels new taxes exempt from the state spending limit. Prop 40 is exempt.
- Prop 43: two-thirds vote for local special taxes that citizens put on the ballot.
- Prop 3 (high-income tax made permanent, $5–15B a year after 2031) belongs to the same group.

This cluster ranks at or near the top on all three lenses. It's also the clearest test of the money-before-mood idea.
